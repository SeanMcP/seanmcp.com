[On a walk through the park](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jVIWSuMgVT8), Matt
Colville rebuked a certain type of RPG fan who complains about the cost of "The
Hobby." These folks turn paying for RPG materials into a moral issue where
spending money is a failure, and Colville is right to push back. Within reason,
it's not wrong to enjoy good things in life with the abundance we've been
given^[Read _Ecclesiastes_ for a more thorough meditation].

But while addressing some of the complaints about monetizing The Hobby, Colville
misses what I consider to be the core issue: the expectations it sets for new
players.

Someone walks into Barnes & Nobel, sees three beautiful hardcovers from Dungeons
& Dragons, and thinks that they need to spend $150 to start playing. Add another
$50 campaign book and a few extra sets of dice for friends, and they've cleared
$200. Oh, and they're going to need a battle map and a few minis, so let's call
it $250. All of this down before ever rolling a die.

Some might pick up the Starter Set instead (only $55), and know that they can
share dice and skip minis. They're probably not going to know that
[the D&D 5.5e SRD is free](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd), and a basic Chessex
dice set is under $5. The expectation has already been set: RPGs are fun ways to
spend your money.

When I was young, I didn't have $55 to try D&D (let alone $250). My brothers and
I stumbled on a
[DragonStrike box set](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DragonStrike_(board_game)>)
at a yard sale for less than $10^[I don't remember the exact amount, but it was
somewhere around there]. That single purchase sustained us for years, because we
learned quickly that imagination was the only real cost to entry. That's the
expectation we should be setting: RPGs are basically free.

That doesn't mean that people can't choose to spend (a lot of) money on The
Hobby. Good for them, and good for the publishers who produce quality materials.
But they should be the exception and not the rule. The default should be like
drawing or singing: something that you can do with your friends for free.
